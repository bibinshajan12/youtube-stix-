# Stix Long-Form Video Blueprint

**Target length:** 8–12 minutes  
**Format:** 16:9 horizontal  
**Purpose:** Take Stix through 5–10 ranked stages that form one continuous survival story  
**Rule:** Every rank must change Stix’s situation; avoid presenting a disconnected list.

## Before writing

- **Main question:** Can Stix __________?
- **Viewer payoff:** By the end, viewers will know __________.
- **Stix’s mission:** __________
- **Starting resources:** __________
- **Main limitation:** oxygen / time / money / health / distance / tools / lives
- **Ranking measurement:** safest, deadliest, strongest, deepest, fastest, or most survivable
- **Midpoint rule change:** __________
- **Final winner or answer:** __________
- **Fact requiring verification:** __________
- **Next related episode:** __________

## Timeline and script

### 0:00–0:15 — Cold open

Show a moment from the final three ranks without revealing the answer.

**Narration template:**  
“Stix has reached __________. The Stix Meter is almost empty—and the most dangerous level has not started.”

Then cut back to the beginning.

### 0:15–0:40 — Promise, mission, and rules

State what Stix must do, what is being ranked, and how the winner will be decided.

**Narration template:**  
“Stix must cross __________. We will rank every level using __________, __________, and __________. Stix gets only __________.”

Show the complete ranking ladder briefly, but conceal the top three.

### 0:40–1:10 — First payoff

Start the first test immediately. Let Stix make a decision and show the result before adding background information.

**Structure:** Setup → Stix predicts → test → result → updated score.

### 1:10–3:00 — Early ranks

Move quickly through the first stages. Each should take approximately 25–40 seconds.

For every rank:

1. Show the new environment.
2. Give Stix a choice.
3. Reveal one useful fact through action.
4. Update the Stix Meter and ranking board.
5. Introduce the next danger before fully relaxing the tension.

**Open loop:** “Stix thinks __________ is the biggest danger. At level ___, Stix discovers what was missed.”

### 3:00–4:30 — Complication

Take away one advantage: damage Stix’s tool, shorten the timer, block the expected exit, remove power, or change the environment.

**Narration template:**  
“Until now, Stix has depended on __________. From this point forward, that advantage is gone.”

Give Stix a small failure so the episode no longer feels predictable.

### 4:30–5:30 — Midpoint reversal

Reveal that the frightening or expensive option is not necessarily the most dangerous or effective.

**Narration template:**  
“Stix was preparing for __________. But the real threat is __________.”

Use a visual comparison, map, or simulation to prove the reversal.

### 5:30–7:30 — Higher ranks

Slow down slightly and increase production quality. Let Stix apply lessons from the earlier ranks rather than surviving through luck.

Use one callback: an object, rule, mistake, or fact from the first two minutes becomes useful here.

### 7:30–9:30 — Top three

Give each finalist a distinct reason it could win. Avoid presenting the obvious winner too early.

- **Number three:** Strong apparent contender with one hidden weakness.
- **Number two:** Nearly wins and forces Stix to use the last resource.
- **Number one:** Delivers the largest visual and the clearest answer to the title.

Place the best animation, sound design, map shot, or comparison in this section.

### 9:30–10:30 — Final decision and payoff

Stix completes the mission using something established earlier. Display the finished ranking and explain the deciding factor in one or two sentences.

**Narration template:**  
“Stix chooses __________. It wins because __________—not because __________.”

Do not add a new ranking criterion during the final reveal.

### Final 20 seconds — Exit and next episode

Show the immediate consequence of Stix’s decision, then tease a logically connected challenge.

**Narration template:**  
“Stix survived __________. But next time, Stix must __________.”

End immediately after the tease. Avoid repeating the entire ranking or delivering a long subscription speech.

## Retention checklist

- Stix appears during explanations, not only during action scenes.
- The title question is stated within 30 seconds.
- The first result arrives before 60 seconds.
- A new question opens before the current question closes.
- The situation changes around minutes 3 and 5.
- Stix experiences at least one failure.
- The top three receive the strongest visuals.
- The final answer uses the criteria established at the beginning.
- Scientific and historical claims are verified before recording.
- The ending bridges naturally to another Stix adventure.

## Example concept

**Title:** What If Stix Fell to the Bottom of the Ocean?  
**Mission:** Escape a damaged research pod before oxygen reaches zero.  
**Ranks:** Sunlight, twilight, midnight, abyssal, and hadal zones.  
**Resources:** One ballast release, emergency light, radio, and limited oxygen.  
**Complication:** Communication fails and the ballast control becomes damaged.  
**Reversal:** The creatures outside look dangerous, but pressure and equipment failure are the real threats.  
**Callback:** A manual release noticed during the opening becomes Stix’s final escape option.  
**Payoff:** Stix releases the ballast, begins rising, and reveals the depth where rescue had become least realistic.  
**Next episode:** Stix tests which airplane seat gives the best chance in different emergencies.
