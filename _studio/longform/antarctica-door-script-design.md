# STIX Found a Door Beneath Antarctica — Long-Form Script Design

## Format

- **Length:** 10–12 minutes
- **Genre:** Animated survival mystery with science-fiction elements
- **Main character:** Stix
- **Story engine:** Five descending danger levels beneath the ice
- **Primary meters:** Body heat, oxygen, exit time, and door-seal integrity
- **Viewer payoff:** The audience sees what is behind the door and learns why it was sealed.
- **Factual boundary:** Present the door, Project Aster, and underground vault as fiction. Real Antarctic features such as blue ice, subglacial lakes, severe cold, and research methods can provide grounding after verification.

## Core premise

During a fictional Antarctic ice survey, Stix detects a perfectly rectangular heat signal beneath the surface. A storm is approaching, giving Stix less than one hour to investigate and return. Beneath layers of blue ice, Stix finds a sealed metal door marked **ASTER-0**.

The door does not protect treasure from the outside. It is an emergency containment seal protecting a hidden artificial biosphere from the frozen surface—and protecting the surface from whatever has evolved inside.

## Stix’s mission

Stix must:

1. Descend through the ice.
2. Identify the source of the heat signal.
3. Open the mysterious door.
4. Discover what Project Aster was preserving.
5. Reseal the vault before the storm closes the exit and the two environments contaminate each other.

## Mystery questions

Open these questions one at a time and resolve them in this order:

1. Why is there heat beneath the ice?
2. Who built the door?
3. Why is something knocking from the other side?
4. What is Project Aster protecting?
5. Is the door designed to keep Stix out—or keep something else in?

Do not open all five questions during the introduction. Each answer should create the next question.

## Full script flow

### 0:00–0:20 — Cold open: three knocks

**Visual:** Stix stands before a massive metal door embedded in blue ice. The body-heat meter is falling. Frost around the door begins melting from heat escaping through the seam.

Three heavy knocks come from the other side.

The door-seal meter flashes: **CONTAINMENT FAILURE — 18%**.

**Narration:**

> “Stix is hundreds of metres beneath Antarctica. The exit is freezing shut, the air behind this door is warm—and something has just knocked three times.”

Stix reaches toward the door control.

**Cut to black before it opens.**

**Narration:**

> “Forty-seven minutes earlier…”

**Retention purpose:** Show the promised door immediately, establish a physical threat, and open the “what knocked?” question.

---

### 0:20–0:55 — Mission and deadline

**Visual:** Stix operates a small ice-survey vehicle during a whiteout. A scanner shows an unnatural rectangle of warmth beneath the ice.

**Narration:**

> “Stix came to map the ice. Instead, the scanner found a shape nature almost never makes: a perfect rectangle, warmer than everything around it.”

The weather system displays: **WHITEOUT ARRIVES IN 58:00**.

Stix has:

- One thermal suit
- One oxygen cylinder
- One cable winch
- One mapping drone
- Fifty-eight minutes to return

**Stix’s choice:** Mark the location and leave, or investigate before the storm erases the entrance. Stix chooses to descend.

**Retention purpose:** Establish the rules and make Stix responsible for the decision.

---

### 0:55–1:50 — Level 1: the whiteout

**Visual:** Wind intensifies while Stix anchors the cable and cuts an opening into the ice. The Stix Meter tracks heat and return time.

The mapping drone briefly detects a weak repeating signal: three pulses, a pause, then three more.

**Narration:**

> “The heat signal was strange. The repeating signal was worse. Something below was still using power.”

Stix attaches the cable and begins descending just as snow starts covering the vehicle.

**Mini-payoff:** The strange pulses match the three knocks from the cold open, but their source remains unknown.

---

### 1:50–2:55 — Level 2: the blue-ice shaft

**Visual:** Stix descends through layered blue ice. Trapped bubbles and dark bands pass behind Stix like a timeline.

The temperature begins rising slightly instead of falling.

The cable catches against a sharp ice ledge. Stix has to disconnect briefly, swing past the obstruction, and reconnect below it.

**Narration:**

> “Every metre should have taken Stix farther from the surface warmth. Instead, the air was getting warmer.”

Stix sees a straight black line beneath the ice. The line becomes the top edge of a metal structure.

**End beat:** The scanner identifies an empty chamber around it.

**Retention purpose:** Answer the heat question partially—there is an artificial structure—but open the larger question of who built it.

---

### 2:55–3:50 — Level 3: the door

**Visual:** Stix lands on a narrow platform before a circular door. It is scratched but not ancient. A faded label reads:

**PROJECT ASTER — VAULT 0**  
**EMERGENCY CONTAINMENT**

The door has three mechanical locks and no active screen.

Warm water runs beneath the platform and disappears into the ice.

**Narration:**

> “This was not an entrance built for visitors. It was a seal built for emergencies.”

Stix finds a warning symbol showing two environments separated by the door: frozen air on one side, a warm green chamber on the other.

**Viewer prediction moment:**

> “Would you open it—or leave before the storm seals the way back?”

Stix notices the repeated three-pulse signal coming from a powerless maintenance panel.

---

### 3:50–4:55 — The three-lock sequence

Each lock should require a different action, creating three fast mini-payoffs.

#### Lock 1 — Heat

Stix uses a suit-heating patch to thaw the frozen mechanical wheel.

#### Lock 2 — Pressure

The wheel will not move until Stix equalizes pressure using a manual valve. The oxygen meter drops while the chamber fills.

#### Lock 3 — Pattern

The maintenance panel flashes the same three-pulse sequence heard above. Stix copies the sequence instead of forcing the final lock.

The door begins opening inward.

**Narration:**

> “Stix had assumed the signal was a distress call. It was actually the door’s emergency access code.”

**Retention purpose:** Resolve the pulse mystery but immediately reveal that the knocking has a different source.

---

### 4:55–5:40 — Midpoint reveal: warm air

**Visual:** Warm mist pours through the opening. Ice cracks around the frame as the temperature difference attacks the seal.

Beyond the door is a narrow metal corridor filled with vines, condensation, and emergency lights that still glow faintly.

Fresh three-part knocking comes from deeper inside.

The door-seal meter activates: **82% — FALLING**.

**Narration:**

> “Stix had opened the door. But the door had also started opening the ice.”

Stix now has two deadlines:

- The storm will close the surface route.
- The warming doorway will destabilize the chamber.

**Midpoint reversal:** Opening the door is not the victory. It creates the episode’s real survival problem.

---

### 5:40–6:50 — Level 4: the abandoned maintenance ring

**Visual:** Stix moves through a curved corridor surrounding a much larger chamber. Broken observation windows show flashes of green below.

Stix finds a fictional Project Aster archive:

- It was built as an isolated backup ecosystem.
- It preserved plants, microbes, water, and climate records.
- The facility sealed itself when its human operators evacuated.
- The inner environment has continued changing without them.

Avoid explaining everything in one narration block. Reveal each point through signs, diagrams, and Stix’s actions.

**Setback:** Stix’s cable goes slack. The upper shaft has shifted, and the original ascent route is partly blocked.

**Narration:**

> “Stix had found the answer to who built the vault. Now the problem was getting out of it.”

---

### 6:50–7:45 — What was knocking?

**Visual:** Stix follows the sound to a damaged maintenance drone repeatedly striking a jammed pipe: three impacts, a pause, then three impacts.

The drone is not calling Stix. It is trying to close the failing seal.

When Stix frees it, the drone projects a warning:

**BIOSPHERE EXPOSURE**  
**OUTER SEAL MUST CLOSE**

**Narration:**

> “There was no monster behind the door. The knocking was a machine trying to undo Stix’s mistake.”

**Retention purpose:** Resolve the opening mystery while escalating the containment problem.

---

### 7:45–8:45 — Level 5: the biosphere vault

**Visual centerpiece:** The maintenance ring opens onto a vast artificial cavern containing a lake, plants, mist, and unfamiliar but non-monstrous life. A false sun glows weakly above it.

Stix is visibly amazed, but the Stix Meter detects particles crossing the open door in both directions.

The archive explains the crucial truth:

> “The door was not protecting treasure. It was protecting two environments from each other.”

The hidden ecosystem could be damaged by surface contamination, while unfamiliar organisms or microbes could also threaten the outside environment.

**Viewer payoff:** Show the full world behind the door. Do not hide it for a sequel.

---

### 8:45–9:50 — Stix’s final choice

Stix sees a glowing sample container beside the lake. Taking it would prove what was found, but the drone marks it as unsafe to remove.

The surface timer reads eight minutes.

Stix must choose:

1. Take the sample and risk contamination.
2. Explore deeper and lose the exit.
3. Leave the sample, repair the seal, and escape with only the archive data.

Stix chooses the third option.

**Narration:**

> “The greatest discovery of Stix’s life was one Stix could not bring home.”

Stix connects the maintenance drone to the inner controls while manually turning the frozen outer wheel.

**Callback:** The three-pulse pattern from the beginning becomes the final locking sequence.

---

### 9:50–10:45 — Climax: seal and escape

Intercut three meters:

- Door seal rising
- Stix’s oxygen falling
- Surface route closing

The door stops at 96%. A vine or cable is trapped in the frame. Stix crosses back, cuts it free, and rolls through the narrowing gap.

The door closes. The heat leak stops. The ice becomes silent.

Stix climbs through the partly collapsed shaft as the storm reaches the survey vehicle.

The cable anchor begins slipping, but the maintenance drone—now inside the sealed vault—tightens the inner cable system long enough for Stix to reach the surface.

**Important:** Stix survives because of a choice and a callback, not luck alone.

---

### 10:45–11:25 — Payoff and final clue

Inside the vehicle, Stix reviews the recovered archive data. The audience receives clear answers:

- The heat came from the artificial biosphere.
- Project Aster built the door as an environmental containment seal.
- The knocking came from a maintenance drone.
- Stix resealed the vault and left its ecosystem untouched.

Then one final screen appears:

**ASTER NETWORK**  
**VAULT 0: SEALED**  
**VAULTS 1–6: STATUS UNKNOWN**

Six dim signals appear in different extreme environments around the fictional world map.

**Narration:**

> “Stix found one door beneath Antarctica. According to the archive, six more were built—and one of them has just started transmitting.”

End on one flashing location. Do not explain it yet.

## Retention map

| Time | Question opened or answered |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | What is knocking behind the door? |
| 0:25 | Why is there heat under the ice? |
| 1:20 | What is sending the repeating signal? |
| 2:55 | Who built the door? |
| 4:45 | Signal answered: it is the access pattern. |
| 5:00 | What is inside, and why is the seal failing? |
| 6:50 | Knocking answered: a maintenance drone. |
| 7:45 | The vault and biosphere are fully revealed. |
| 8:45 | Will Stix take proof or protect the vault? |
| 10:15 | Stix reseals it and escapes. |
| 11:10 | New series question: where are the other vaults? |

## Visual priorities

Spend the most animation effort on:

1. The first view of the door inside blue ice.
2. Warm mist escaping when the door opens.
3. The full artificial biosphere reveal.
4. The three-meter climax during resealing.
5. The final Aster network map.

Simpler scenes can use:

- Stix’s survey vehicle interior
- Close-ups of the Stix Meter
- Map and diagram explanations
- Silhouettes during the descent
- Reusable corridor backgrounds

## Writing and pacing rules

- Keep Stix on screen during exposition.
- Give the first concrete discovery before 90 seconds.
- Reveal the door before minute three and open it near the midpoint.
- Answer the title completely by showing what is behind the door.
- Use the final network signal as a sequel hook, not as a substitute for the main payoff.
- Avoid presenting Project Aster or the vault as a real Antarctic discovery.
- Verify any real claims about Antarctic conditions before recording.
- Keep creatures mysterious but not automatically evil.
- Let Stix cause the containment problem, take responsibility, and solve it.

## Packaging direction

**Title:** STIX Found a Door Beneath Antarctica

**Thumbnail composition:**

- Stix in the lower-left foreground, small for scale
- Massive warm-orange door embedded in blue ice
- One thin beam of green light escaping through the opening
- A shadow or maintenance-drone shape barely visible inside
- Optional text: **DO NOT OPEN**

The thumbnail should show the door but not the biosphere. The video itself must deliver that reveal.
