# Stix Gets a Job — 20 Workplace Satire Shorts

## Series promise

Stix enters a new workplace in every episode. He barely understands the job, but he understands confidence, visibility, and corporate language. Twig, his capable junior, identifies the real problem and quietly fixes it. Senior management credits Stix, then questions Twig for the exact action that saved the day.

These are fictional workplace-comedy scripts. Each episode is designed for a roughly 50-second vertical Short and uses a concrete, visually understandable problem rather than generic office banter.

## Recurring visual language

- **Stix Promotion Meter:** rises whenever Stix confidently renames a problem.
- **Twig Burnout Meter:** rises whenever Twig fixes something without receiving credit.
- **Green jargon captions:** translate Stix's impressive phrase into its useless real meaning.
- **Red reality captions:** show the operational problem Twig is trying to explain.
- Put Stix and the crisis in the first frame. Do not open on a logo.
- Keep the final call to action after the management punchline, never before the payoff.

## Release-order recommendation

Start with the most instantly legible problems and strongest visual contrasts:

1. AI Strategy Lead — duplicated data creates 900% “growth.”
2. HR Director — weekend overtime disappears.
3. Restaurant Consultant — 42 orders never reach the kitchen.
4. Management Consultant — seventy slides versus one dusty sensor.
5. Cybersecurity Director — everyone is locked out.
6. Retail Director — empty racks, full warehouse, snow outside.
7. Advertising Director — billboard sends customers to a competitor.
8. Bank Risk Manager — every card declines.
9. Hotel Manager — sold out with 40 empty rooms.
10. Construction Manager — wrong beam under the crane.

Use the remaining ten after the audience clearly recognizes the Stix/Twig pattern. Rotate industries and avoid publishing two screen-heavy office episodes back-to-back.

## CTA rotation rule

Every script places its CTA after the punchline. Keep the spoken CTA to one sentence, rotate the wording, and always ask for a specific next job. Viewer suggestions can then become later episodes, making the comment prompt feel consequential rather than decorative.
